 The brain of DigiT: enterprise intelligence that perceives, reasons & acts · v1.0

 AI Intelligence is not another chatbox bolted onto a module. It is a brain — wired into every module in DigiT,
 running a continuous perceive → reason → act loop. A chatbot is pull: you ask, it answers, it sleeps. A brain
 is a loop that never stops: it watches every module, reasons across all of them, and surfaces what matters

 line: the first AI that understands your entire operation — not a corner of it — and acts on it.

 How the brain works — the three phases
 Each phase maps onto an engine you are already building.

 Perceive — it has senses into everything
 Two channels. It subscribes to the cross-workspace event bus (Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY / Supabase
 Realtime), so the instant anything happens — a cert lapses, a deal moves, a metric crosses a line, an asset
 goes quiet — the brain knows. And it has scoped read access to every module's tables (the same
 tenant_id + RLS boundary as everything else), so it can pull the full picture on demand. It runs both ways:
 reactively on events, and on a schedule (cron) for periodic sweeps.

 Reason — where the scripts, commands & scores live
 The brain keeps a continuously-updated operational graph: the entities across workspaces and the links
 between them (this cert → these assets → these batches → this deal → this customer → this revenue). On
 top of it, it runs a library of analytical jobs — compute the DigiT Score, run an anomaly scan, fit a forecast,
 trace a causal chain, run a benchmark, simulate a scenario. The LLM core decides which to run, composes
 them, and — critically — ranks what it finds by business impact, so it surfaces the three things that
 matter, not forty alerts.

 Act — it does not just report, it does
 Within guardrails you set (a confidence threshold, scoped permissions, human-in-the-loop), it surfaces the
 insight and can trigger the fix through the Workflow engine — create the task, draft the message, open the
 CAPA, reschedule the maintenance. Below the confidence line it asks first; above it, it acts and logs. That is

 What makes it a real intelligence, not a one-trick
 • It works the night shift — not a box you query — a brain that watches 24/7 and hands you the 3 things that
    matter each morning, ranked by impact. It earns trust by being right about what is important.
 • It runs the simulation before you commit — “simulate: we take the Globex contract” → it models the load on
    your resources, the compliance implications, the cash flow and the team capacity, and tells you what breaks
    before you sign. A flight simulator for decisions.
 • It sees around corners — it learns your operation's patterns and warns weeks ahead (“~3 weeks from a
    stockout that threatens the Acme contract”). The value is not the alert — it is the lead time to prevent it.
 • It can actually do the work — within your guardrails, specialised agents act — a compliance watchdog that
    opens CAPAs, a follow-up agent that drafts and (on approval) sends, a maintenance scheduler. AI staff with
    scoped permissions and a confidence threshold.
 • You command it by intent — “get me ready for the ISO audit next month” → it assembles the evidence, flags
    the gaps, creates the tasks and drafts the responses — orchestrating across workspaces from one sentence.


 Why the moat deepens over time
 • It learns your specific business — not a generic model — the longer you use it, the more it knows your
    suppliers, your cycles, your customers.
 • Anonymised network effect — because DigiT is multi-tenant, it can tell a client “businesses like yours that
    fixed this early kept the deal 80% of the time” — a data-network effect a single-company tool can never have.
 • It always shows its work — every insight ships with the causal chain and the underlying evidence. The chain
    is the explanation — which is why this design earns trust instead of demanding it. No founder acts on a black
    box when real money is on the line.
