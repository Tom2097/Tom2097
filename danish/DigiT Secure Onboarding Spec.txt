 DigiT — Secure Onboarding & Sign-in
 A four-stage, passwordless, device-bound sign-up · v1.0

 DigiT onboards a new client through four secure stages — identity, company, role and access — and
 deliberately uses no client-chosen email/password. Instead, DigiT generates a one-time alphanumeric
 passcode, emails it to the registered company address, and the user enters it once to lock DigiT to that
 device. The code is consumed and device-bound, so it can never be reused or replayed on another device. If
 the code is lost, sign-in falls back to SSO (Google, Microsoft, Apple or GitHub). The result is a passwordless,
 phishing-resistant model where access is tied to a verified person, a verified company, and a trusted device.

 The four sign-up stages, in detail
 Stage 1 — Your identity (the person)
    Collected
    Full name, work email (must be a company address), phone number (for 2FA & alerts), and a mandatory photo,
    plus basic person details.
    What happens
    You complete a short form and capture a live photo on screen. This work email becomes the address the
    one-time access code is later sent to.
    Secured by
    Free / disposable email domains are blocked. The photo is a live selfie with liveness detection (not a static
    upload), so it proves a real person and cannot be a borrowed image — optionally matched to a government ID for
    KYC-grade assurance.

 Stage 2 — Your company (registered & verified)
    Collected
    Company legal name, registration number (CIN / GST / local equivalent), country, registered address, and website.
    What happens
    DigiT verifies the company is a real registered entity by checking it against official business registries (e.g.
    MCA / CIN and GST in India), and confirms the website / email domain belongs to it.
    Secured by
    Registry verification rejects unregistered or shell entities. A domain-ownership check plus a requirement that the
    Stage 1 email domain matches the verified company domain stops someone registering a company they have
    no connection to.

 Stage 3 — Your role (position & authority)
    Collected
    The person's position / title in the company and their authority to represent or onboard it.
    What happens
    DigiT binds the verified person to the verified company through the matching email domain. Higher-privilege
    roles (owner / admin) can require an extra approval, such as confirmation from an existing company admin.
    Secured by
    Domain-authorization binding, role-based provisioning, and rate-limiting / anomaly checks that block mass or
    abusive sign-ups.

 Stage 4 — Secure access (the passwordless credential)
    Collected
    Nothing typed by you except the one-time code. No password is ever created or stored.
    What happens
    DigiT generates a one-time alphanumeric passcode and emails it to your company address. You enter it once
    on this device, which enrolls and locks DigiT to this device (a device-bound credential / passkey is created). You
    also link one or more SSO backups (Google / Microsoft / Apple / GitHub).
    Secured by
    The code is one-time and consumed the moment it is used; it binds to this one device and can never be
    replayed or used elsewhere (anti-replay); no password exists to steal; and SSO is enrolled as the recovery path.

 The authentication model — how sign-in works
 No password is ever chosen or typed. Access depends on a trusted device, with SSO as the fallback.




  Figure 2 — The sign-in decision: a recognized device signs in silently; an unrecognized device hits a deliberate dead-end, with SSO (or
                                                 re-enrolment) as the only way forward.
 • Trusted (enrolled) device — you are signed in silently via the device credential and an optional biometric —
    you never type an email or password.
 • The one-time code — generated once, a mix of letters and numbers, emailed to your company address, used
    once at enrolment, bound to that one device, and never reusable.
 • A new / unrecognized device — you see only the standard email + password screen — and because you
    have no password, there is no way through, and there is deliberately no field to re-enter the one-time code
    (this is the anti-replay protection). Your options are to sign in with a linked SSO provider, or to sign up again.
 • If the code is lost or cannot be retrieved — DigiT presents the SSO dropdown (Google / Microsoft / Apple /
    GitHub); you pick one, and from then on that SSO method is your sign-in.

 Stepwise summary — for better understanding
 1. Sign up → Stage 1: enter your details and capture a live photo.
 2. Stage 2: enter your registered company; DigiT verifies it against official registries.
 3. Stage 3: state your position; DigiT confirms your email domain matches the verified company.
 4. Stage 4: DigiT emails a one-time alphanumeric code to your company address; you enter it once, which locks
    DigiT to this device, and you link an SSO backup.
 5. From now on, on this device, you are signed in automatically — no password, secured by the device
    credential and biometric.
 6. The code is now used up — it will not work again, and not on any other device.
 7. On a new device, you will see only an email + password screen (which you do not have), with no field for the
    code — use your linked SSO option, or sign up again.
 8. Lost the code? DigiT shows the SSO dropdown (Google / Microsoft / Apple / GitHub); pick a provider and that
    becomes your sign-in method.


 Security & practical notes
 To make this robust in production — recommendations on top of your design.
 • Implement device binding as passkeys / WebAuthn — the phishing-resistant, hardware-backed way — so the
    one-time code creates a real cryptographic device credential, not just a flag. A device-bound key in secure
    storage is the fallback.
 • Make the photo a live selfie with liveness detection — this is the anti-spoof version; pair it with ID matching if
    you need KYC-grade identity assurance.
 • Have a manual-review fallback for company verification — registry coverage varies (MCA / GST in India;
    equivalents elsewhere) — allow a human check for entities an API cannot confirm.
 • Consider softening the new-device dead-end for legitimate users — leading with SSO, and offering a fresh
    one-time code to the company email (email possession is itself strong proof), avoids fully locking out someone
    who simply switched devices — while still blocking anyone who only intercepted the old code. Your strict version
    is the most locked-down; this is the more forgiving variant.
 • Standard hardening — log every auth event, rate-limit attempts, encrypt in transit and at rest, keep tenant
    isolation (RLS), and require the SSO account's email to match the verified company domain.
