 DigiT — Smart Customer CRM
 From a single “Add Leads” tab to an intelligent, end-to-end CRM · v1.0

 Scope: this document covers the Customer CRM only. The Investor CRM shares the same underlying
 engine and is specced separately. Today the module has one tab — Add Leads — a front door with no
 rooms behind it. The build runs in two passes: first the journey a lead travels (making it a real CRM), then
 the intelligence on every step (making it smart) — plus a few bets only DigiT can pull off because the CRM
 is wired into the wider platform.




 Figure 1 — The lead-to-customer journey, the smart layer that sits across every stage, and the auto-provision handoff into the
 platform at “Won.”


 Pass 1 — Build the rooms: the lead-to-customer journey
 Each becomes a tab / section the lead flows through.
 • Lead Inbox & Triage — where new leads land, auto-scored and auto-assigned, sorted by who is worth your
    time today — the screen you open every morning.
 • Contacts & Accounts — the relationship graph: many people per company, org hierarchy, and every
    touchpoint in one place (a lead is a person; an account is the company behind them).
 • Pipeline / Deal Board — a kanban of stages (New → Qualified → Demo → Proposal → Won/Lost) with value
    and probability per deal — the heart of a CRM, and the missing piece today.
 • Engagement Timeline — every email, call, meeting and WhatsApp auto-logged against the contact, so context
    is never lost weeks later.
 • Tasks & Cadences — follow-up reminders and sequences (day 1 email → day 3 nudge → day 7 call) so leads
    never go cold by accident.
 • Communication Hub — send email and WhatsApp from inside the CRM with reusable templates — essential
    for India, where deals close on WhatsApp.
 • Quotes & Proposals — generate a quote straight from the Starter / Growth / Enterprise pricing, send it, and
    track when it is opened.
 • Customer Success — what happens after Won: onboarding checklist, account health, renewal dates and
    upsell flags.
 • Forecast & Reports — pipeline value, conversion by stage, win/loss reasons and revenue projection.

 Pass 2 — Make every room intelligent: the “smart” layer
 This is what separates it from a plain CRM.
 • AI lead scoring — predicts which leads will convert from their signals, so the inbox sorts itself by priority.
 • Auto-enrichment + AI research (“AI SDR”) — paste a name or email and it fills in company size, vertical and
    a short brief (recent news, what they do), so you walk into every call prepared.
 • Duplicate detection & data hygiene — auto-merges duplicates and flags stale records — the #1 reason
    CRMs rot is messy data.
 • Next-best-action — tells you the move: “this deal has gone quiet for 9 days, send a check-in”; “this account is
    ready for an upsell.”
 • Conversation intelligence — summarises calls and email threads and extracts action items and sentiment
    automatically.
 • AI drafting — writes the follow-up, proposal or re-engagement email in your voice; you edit and send.
 • Deal-health radar — flags at-risk deals and explains why (“no decision-maker engaged”, “stalled at proposal”)
    before they slip.
 • Natural-language ask-your-CRM — “which deals close this month and which are shaky?” → answer + chart,
    no report-building.


 Pass 3 — The creative bets that are yours alone
 • WhatsApp-first capture & nurture — capture leads from WhatsApp and run nurture sequences via the
    WhatsApp Business API — the highest-leverage CRM feature in India, and rarely native in enterprise CRMs.
 • Vertical-aware intelligence — the CRM knows pharma vs renewables vs logistics and surfaces vertical buying
    signals (a new pharma facility, a solar developer winning a SECI auction, a logistics firm expanding fleet).
 • “Design Partner” pipeline mode — a pipeline tuned for founder-led early sales — track the give-to-get,
    reference status and feedback alongside the deal.
 • Buying-signal radar — monitors triggers (a contact changes jobs, the company raises funding, expansion
    news) and pings you when it is the moment to reach out.
 • Lead-source ROI — shows which channel actually converts (LinkedIn vs waitlist vs referrals) so you double
    down on what works — feeding your GTM.
 • Founder's daily briefing — a one-screen AI digest each morning: the 5 leads to chase today and why, deals
    that went quiet, proposals opened twice yesterday.


 The unfair advantage — wire the CRM into the platform
 When a deal hits Won, the CRM does not just update a field — it auto-provisions the new customer:
 creates their tenant, kicks off the onboarding workflow (via the Workflow engine), and starts feeding their
 data into the Performance and Resources workspaces. Customer CRM and Investor CRM share one engine,
 and every account carries a single relationship score — the CRM equivalent of the DigiT Score. A
 standalone CRM ends at “Won”; yours hands the customer straight into the operational-intelligence loop.
 That is the story that justifies the price over any point-tool CRM.

 Suggested build order
 To make “Add Leads” a CRM you can run design-partner sales on before launch, add these first, in order:
    1. Pipeline / Deal Board + Contacts & Accounts — without a pipeline you do not have a CRM. This is the
    foundation everything else hangs on.
    2. Engagement Timeline + Tasks & Cadences — so leads stop going cold — the highest-ROI behaviour
    change.
    3. Communication Hub (email + WhatsApp) — reach prospects where they actually are in India.
    4. AI lead scoring + next-best-action — the first true “smart” features — they make the daily inbox
    useful.
    5. Auto-provision on Won — the platform handoff — turns closing a deal into onboarding a tenant.

 Everything beyond step 5 (vertical-aware signals, buying-signal radar, conversation intelligence, founder's briefing) is high-value
 but post-launch — layer it on once the core journey is proven with real deals.
